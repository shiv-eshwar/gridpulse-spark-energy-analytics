GridPulse research scope and publication gate

Research question: How do seasonal conditioning and event-time lateness budgets
jointly affect detection sensitivity, retained evidence, and state cost in a
reproducible Spark energy-telemetry pipeline?

Completed assignment-scale experiments:
1. Global, hour-of-day, and hour-of-week median/MAD baselines.
2. Chronological train (2006-2008), calibration (2009), test (2010).
3. Point spikes and six-hour shifts at +1, +2, +4 kW; three seeds.
4. Threshold at calibration score 99th percentile (approximate quantile).
5. Pointwise confusion counts; no point-adjustment inflation.
6. Watermark semantics ablation: 0, 30 and 180 minutes.
7. Full valid-source partition microbenchmark; three randomized repeats.
8. Checkpoint restart during 21,600-real-record replay and batch parity.

What is NOT established:
- New anomaly detection algorithm, causality, electricity theft detection.
- Real fault accuracy (dataset lacks natural anomaly ground truth).
- Distributed cluster scalability (the executed environment is local[4]).
- Statistical significance from three synthetic seeds.
- Conference novelty or acceptance probability.

Publication gate before submitting a paper:
- Add independently labelled multi-household datasets (e.g. a suitable licensed
  energy anomaly benchmark); audit label origin and household-level splits.
- Add persistence, seasonal forecasting residual, Isolation Forest and a
  strong recent anomaly baseline under the same compute and calibration budget.
- Couple faults to delayed arrivals, compare online alerts to offline reference,
  measure detection delay, event-level precision/recall, memory and state cost.
- Test random seeds and delay distributions with paired day-block bootstrap
  confidence intervals; retain untouched households for final validation.
- Vary source rates and run on a real multi-executor cluster, report hardware,
  resources, shuffled bytes, skew, state growth and checkpoint recovery time.
- Compare to verified related work and articulate an original contribution.
- Use a second dataset and negative findings to challenge the hypothesis.

Additional completed experiment: the same 21,600 real readings are replayed with
every tenth measurement delayed by one 3,600-reading file (~60 source hours).
Two-hour, 72-hour and 168-hour watermarks measure retention, hourly energy error, alert
changes, and observed peak state memory. This tests one deterministic delayed
arrival schedule; broad distributional and alert-delay evaluation remains open.

Final revision adds explicit streaming eligibility and finite-value checks,
pinned atomic source recovery, monthly coverage and missing-run diagnostics,
key-set assertions, completed Spark event-log exports, local data-volume runs
with warmups, and a joint raw-minute fault/lateness experiment. The joint protocol
uses 12 random eligible hours and synthetic +10 kW short bursts on every tenth
minute (seed 2026). It compares final snapshots with the independent offline
reference. This does not replace the publication gates above: real fault labels,
uncertainty under broader delay distributions, strong external baselines and
multi-executor evaluation remain open research work.
